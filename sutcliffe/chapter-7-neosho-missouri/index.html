CHAPTER 7 – NEOSHO, MISSOURI September 4, 1919 – October 17, 1919 Neosho, Missouri September 4, 1919 My dearest Hazel, I had intended to write you last night, but I got in late and the bed looked so inviting. Well, I am located here and I have started to work. I left my room at 6:30 a.m. yesterday and didn’t get back until 11:30 last night, so you see, I put in a full day, and I had a splendid business. I am going about 6 miles south of town and I don’t think I will be back till tomorrow noon. I am coming up on the train Friday evening. With love Carl Neosho, Missouri September 9, 1919 Dearest Hazel, Just a few lines to you today to tell you that I am going to Kansas City on the midnight train tonight after my car, and I expect to be back here Thursday evening. It is to awful hot to walk in the country. I went out all day yesterday and had a pretty good day, and I started out again this morning, but I had to come back. I was afraid of getting overheated. I got too warm yesterday and I am not feeling so very good. I will get in to K.C. 8 o’clock in the morning and I am going to try to get away early Thursday morning, and if I have good luck, I should get in here by evening. With love from Carl Neosho, Missouri September 18, 1919 Dearest Hazel, I had intended to write you a short letter last night, but I got interested in a magazine and before I knew it, it was 11 o’clock and I went to bed. I have been busy as usual, working pretty hard all week. I have been blessed with tire trouble the last few days and laid up on the road a good deal. I am not quite as optimistic about using a car in this country as I was and I am not sure that it is going to prove a success in the long run. I have been working entirely on large orders this week. The last 2 days I have been between Neosho and Seneca. You may look for me Saturday evening. The road to Seneca is pretty bad, and I think I will come on the train. With love from Carl Kansas City, Missouri September 24, 1919 [Note: Postcard showing entrance to Swope Park, Kansas City, Missouri] Am stopping here an hour changing train for Louisiana. Leave here 8 p.m. Think I will be back Friday or Saturday. C. St. Louis, Missouri September 25, 1919 [Note: Postcard showing the Zoo at Forest Park in St. Louis, Missouri.] On my way back. Leave here 9 p.m.. Arrive Friday 10 a.m. C. Neosho, Missouri October 2, 1919 My dearest Hazel, Your letter came this morning and I was so glad to have it. And I am going to write you a few lines so you can get it tomorrow. I have been in Granby today and just got back. I am taking it a litle easy today for I was pretty tired. I walked about 30 miles yesterday. I broke a spring in the car. I have had just a splendid week so far, and there is still two days left, and I expect to break the record this week. I got the thread for you and I will bring it over Saturday evening. I am coming over on the train. With love to you, dearest Carl Neosho, Missouri October 15, 1919 Dearest Hazel, I have just come in from the country and I am a litle tired. I am leaving here on the train tomorrow morning at 10:02 for Kansas City and I expect to be back some time Sunday. This is such awful brief letter dear, but I haven’t a thing to write about. I will drop you a line again in a day or two. With all my love, dear Carl Kansas City, Missouri October 17, 1919 Dearest Hazel, Just a few lines to tell you I arrived in good shape and I have been around town all day shaking hands with old friends. Am having a good time. I am going to the Slippery Gulch tonight with Dr. Gist and Sid Houston. I am going to stay with Dr. Gist tonight. I expect to start back tomorrow evening or Sunday. I am lonesome for the hills already. All my love to you, dear Carl Neosho, Missouri October 21, 1919 Dearest Hazel, I came back here early yesterday morning, and I had intended to write you last night, but I went to the country as soon as I came in and I got soaking wet, and I was pretty tired last night. I have been at McElheiny (?) today and I am going there again tomorrow, and I may stay there two or three days. I am doing just fine. I had a pretty good time in Kansas City, but I am mighty glad to be back after all. I don’t think there is any place as nice as these Missouri hills, and I don’t ever intend to leave them. I am getting dreadful anxious to see you, dear, and of course, I will be up there Saturday evening. With all my love to you, dearest. Carl
