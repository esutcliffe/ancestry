Chapter 9 – Chanute, Kansas April 24, 1924 – December 16, 1936 Bartlesville, Oklahoma April 24, 1924 Dearest Hazel – children, Just got your letter and I called Weston to find out what he had at Chanute. He called Chanute and they said for me to come up and see them, so I am leaving now. It is 100 miles from here. Will write you as soon as I get there. All my love to all of you. Carl Chanute, Kansas April 24, 1924 Dearest Hazel and children, I arrived here this afternoon 4 o’clock. I made pretty good time. It is 90 miles from Bartlesville. I went right out to the cement factory and met Mr. Gardner, the Superintendent. This is a mighty interesting place. They manufactured 4,500 barrels of cement every day, and employ 450 people. In addition to that, they own 1,200 acres of land, operate a dairy farm, milk 40 Guernseys, a hog farm of 600 head, and a poultry farm, and at the present time, they have 10,000 chicks weighing about a pound each. What they intend to do now is to start out landscaping this ground. They have about 25 acres laid off for lawn and schrubbery border, and want to beautify it as well as around the buildings on the farms, also plant some small orchards around on the farms. This is just one of several branch factories scattered out over the country, and they have been studying on this for several years, but they never have been able to get appropriations from the main office until this year. They have some mighty good ideas. Their place is 3 miles out of town, but we would have to live in town. They think real soon they would build a house and greenhouse out there. I haven’t met the main guy yet. Am to meet him in the morning. I don’t know if I can make a deal with them. Am going to try my best and I am going to ask them to give me $150.00 per month and furnish me a car. Chanute is a pretty town, about the size of Pittsburg, and you can rent a good 5 room house here for about $25.00 to $28.00. But I am going to bed now and I will tell you all about it tomorrow. All my love to you all. Mighty sorry to hear about your Dad’s accident. Carl Nowata, Oklahoma April 25, 1924 My Dearest Hazel and children, I just had to call you up and tell you the news. I just got in and had my supper, and called you up and now I am going to write you a few lines. I made a deal with the cement people, and I am going up there just as soon as I can. I am to get $150.00 per month. They asked me if it was my car I was driving. I told them it belonged to the Company, and they said they always had a car sitting around, so I am going to sell it then we can pay up our debts and get some furniture. And I hope and pray that this will end my career as a traveling man so I can be with those I love better than anything in the world. Honey, I just can’t stand to be away from you and the babies, and it is not right that I should be. I am going to have a fine job. I am to have full charge of the planting and care of all their stock, and they told me that it didn’t make any difference whether I needed one man or 40. All I had to do is to go to the Superintendent and call for them. Mr. Weston must have given me a good send off to them, for they seemed tickled to get me at the price. The General Manager used to live next door to Mr. Rock in Kansas City and we got acquainted right now. I only felt sorry that I didn’t ask for more money. They indicate that in the future, they may build us a house out on the place, and from what I could observe, living is very reasonable in Chanute. It is not always so easy to find a house there, for there are a good many factory workers living there, but I feel that we should begin to enjoy life more fully now Dear, as I will always be home and then I will be doing something that I like. I am mighty tired. I have driven 100 miles since noon. I couldn’t get through by Bartlesville. The roads are bad, and I am going to have an awful time getting to Tulsa tomorrow. I will be up just as soon as I can. I expect in 3 or 4 days. With all my love to you all, and kiss the babies for me. Carl Chanute, Kansas May 5, 1924 Dearest Hazel – children, I arrived here Sunday evening at 8 o’clock after traveling all day. I missed the train at Pittsburg and had to take a train to Fort Scott, go from there to Iola on a bus, and from Iola to here in another bus, and I arrived here with .50 in my pocket. I put in my first day here and I think I am going to get along just fine. They seem to be very fine people to deal with. They just turn the whole thing over to me and give me all the help I need. I am staying in a boarding house about ¾ mile from where I work, paying $8.00 per week for room and board. I am wondering what you thought of the furniture at Jensens (?). I am going to look around here and see what I can find in the way of a gas stove, but you had better send that sheet to me of Montgomery Ward so in case we decide to get that. I wish you would send me that envelope of cox comb seed I forgot at once. I will not have much time to look for a house for a week or 10 days, and I would also like to get a payday first. I think they pay twice a month. I had to buy me a pair of everyday shoes today $5.00, and a shirt $1.00. I am pretty tired and will close, but will write again in a day or two. Love to you all. Carl 405 West Main Street, Chanute, Kansas Chanute, Kansas May 7, 1924 My Dearest Hazel – children, I am so awful tired tonight, but I will try to write you a few lines, for I know you will be looking for a letter. I feared that if I waited until tomorrow, you would not get it until Monday. If anything, I am more tired tonight than I was last night. I have planted 1,100 schrubs today, and I think I will get through planting tomorrow. Then I can begin to take it easy. But I am feeling fine, for I am doing what I like to do and that makes things a lot easier. I am sure we are going to like it here. It strikes me as a mighty nice place to live. Everybody speaks to you when you meet them on the street. I am going to write Mr. Fortier and see if I can hire him to crate and pack our stuff at Tulsa. It would be cheaper for me to pay him for it than for me to go over there. It is awful cold here and has been for 2 days. I see they are having a snowstorm in Nebraska. This is going to be all for this time. I am going to bed. I get up at 6 every morning. Good night, Dearest. With all my love to all of you. Carl 405 West Main Street Write me soon. Chanute, Kansas May 12, 1924 My Dearest Hazel and children, I have just had my dinner and a fairly good dinner as they run in a boarding house. I had my first dish of strawberries, but they were not good at all. I received your letter Friday evening and was just awful glad to hear from you. Yes, I knew you would have a nice visit in Neosho. You can’t help but enjoy visiting such splendid people as we had for neighbors up there, and I fear that Neosho will always seem more or less like home to us. I am mighty glad that you are pleased with your new furniture, but I hadn’t expected you to ship it so soon. However, it is on the way now and I will either find a way of storing it or rent a house. I want you up here just as soon as possible, but by the time we pay Jensen $106.50 freight on furniture from St. Joseph, house rent, $15.00 deposit on gas, water, and electricity, we will perhaps have to wait until June 1st or 5th. I am sending $2.00 to Neosho Savings Bank today, and I will send the order for the range tomorrow. You didn’t say if you got the check from the nursery. Let me know how you are fixed financially. Be sure to pay your Mother before anyone else. How is your Dad getting along, Honey? I looked at 2 houses this morning, but I didn’t want either one. They were old and just partly modern. We can get a good house here for from $25.00 to $30.00, and I am not going to grab the first thing that comes along. It is not always so easy to find a bungalow here. Would you want anything with an up stairs if we could find something nice? I like my work better every day. I worked mighty hard last week but I am all through planting now, and can begin taking it easy. The Superintendent said yesterday, he didn’t want to see me do any digging. They have plenty of laborers to do that, but I can do more work in 3 hours than 3 of these men do in a day, and I get sick watching them. It is mostly Negroes and Mexicans they use for laborers around here. I know you are going to like it up here. It is an awful pretty town. Not as large as I thought. A litle over 10,000. Just an old fashioned town with the finest schools and churches. You remember the editorials the Star used to write about Kansas. They told the truth in them. If I can get Mr. Fortier to pack our goods at Tulsa, it will save me a trip over there, and I have no desire to go to Tulsa any more. I am better satisfied than I have been in a year here. I miss you and the babies dreadfully, but it will not be long until we will get settled again and I hope it will be for some time. 5 p.m. I have been out for a walk since starting this, and I looked at an awful nice 5 room house. It is for sale, but they said they might rent it. It has a front room, dining room, kitchen, closet, and a toilet down stairs. All very large rooms. And 2 large rooms and large bath room up stairs. It is in the very best of shape, and in the best neighborhood in town, but I know you would rather have a bungalow and so would I. I haven’t sent Jensen a check yet. We won’t have money enough until the 20th of May, but I think I will send him $50.00 now and the rest when I get paid. I am just afraid, Honey, that we can’t get to housekeeping until June 5th. I will try and see if I can see me (?) around. I won’t rent any house until I hear from you. I will close this now. Supper is ready. Write me real soon, Honey. Did the hail hit Neosho? All my love to all of you. Kiss the children for me. Carl Chanute, Kansas May 16, 1924 My Dearest Hazel and children, I haven’t anything new to write about tonight, but I thought I had better send you a few lines or you may not have a letter from me until Monday. I am told it is an awful nice one. Dearest, I am not going to take the first thing that comes along. I want something where we can be comfortable, for we are going to be here for some time. I like my job here better every day. They are mighty fine people to work for. I have lots of work to do, but I have plenty of help, from 2 to 10 men every day. It has been awful cold here almost since I came, but it warmed up some today, and it looks like rain tonight. I haven’t written Fortiers yet. I am going to wait until I have seen that house tomorrow, and if I rent it, and if it has a built in cabinet. Then I am going to have him sell the one we have in Tulsa. But I am going to close this now, and I will write you again in a day or two. Write me often, Honey. I am getting awful hungry to see you and the babies. Kiss them for me. Carl Chanute, Kansas May 19, 1924 My Dearest Hazel – children, This has been a mighty long day. I took a long walk around town this morning, slept 2 hours after dinner, then went to the Public Library and spent a couple of hours, and have just had supper. The folks here have asked me to go to church with them, and I think I will. They are having Baccalaureate Service here. 94 students will graduate here next Thursday. They have the finest schools here you ever saw. I got word today that we can have the house I mentioned in my letter last night. It will be a week or 10 days before it is ready. They have the papering and some painting, the bath fixtures to put in, but I think I can put the furniture in the house in one room next Saturday, and I hope some of the furniture from Neosho and St. Joseph will be here by then. I haven’t any news to tell you this time, and I will close this. I sure hope to hear from you tomorrow. All my love to all of you. Carl Chanute, Kansas May 31, 1924 My Dearest Hazel and children, I told you in my letter last night that I would write you again, and I know you will be waiting for a letter. This has been a lonesome day. I went out to the Plant this morning, and this afternoon, I went out to see if I could locate another house, but I can’t find a thing that I would have. But they dug the hole in the alley yesterday for the wire, and I am sure they will get the wire in to the house tomorrow. Then it will only take 3 or 4 days to finish it up, and meantime, I am sure all our stuff will get here. And I am hoping I can have you come Friday or Saturday, for I know I will go nutty if I have to live in this boarding house much longer. It is a typical boarding house, and the grub is not very appetizing. I eat my lunch out at the Plant. They serve lunch for the office force and the foremen, and they serve a good meal for .30. That relieves the monotony somewhat from the boarding house style. And tonight I went down town for my supper. So you had better hold yourself in readiness to come anytime after Thursday. I don’t want to have to spend another Sunday after this next one away from you and the babies. I think the living room and dining room in the house are 13 X 17, and the 2 bedrooms 11 X 13. That is why I like the house. Most of the houses for rent here are very old and the rooms are so small, or there is an upstairs to them. This house I have rented was the only one I have seen that I wanted. They put a dandy kitchen cabinet in yesterday, about 3 times as roomy as the one in Metcalf’s house. I am going to close this now and take it to the Post Office so you will get it tomorrow. You asked me in one of your letters how I was feeling. Physically, I am feeling better than I have in a long time and my nerves are so much better. That was what was bothering me. I got to worrying, and worried until it affected my physical condition, but I am feeling mighty good now and gaining weight every day. With all my love to all of you. Carl Chanute, Kansas June 2, 1924 My Dearest Hazel and children, I have just come back from town and supper. The folks went to the country today and I took my meals down town. It was a nice change. They had come back when I did, but I was glad I had eaten already. I have been up to the house all day. The gas range came yesterday and the goods from Tulsa came Friday, and I have everything in the house. The stove is awful nice and if it will cook as good as it looks, it will be a cracker jack. Mrs. Fortier wrote that Mr. Fortier had a sore hand, and they had to have Mr. Morris crate and pack it. He charged $11.00 for the work. Storage was $4.00 and freight was $9.50. IT sure cost money to move. I have unpacked everything but 2 barrels and everything was all right. But of course, the dishes are in the barrels. Don’t worry about the furniture, Honey. I have it all locked up in the front bed room and it will stay there until the front and living room are finished, and I am not going to let anybody move it unless I am there, and it is all covered with canvas and blankets and no dust can get to it. I told the owner of the home today to have it ready by Saturday a.m., or I would move out again, and I think it will be ready. You get ready and come Saturday a.m. Your train leaves Joplin 8:40 a.m. and reaches Chanute 12:05, and I will have a place ready for you. I don’t want to move out of this house. It suits me better than any place I have seen here. I mailed the bag the same day I sent your dress, and had it insured, and that reminds me. I had a letter from the insurance company today to the effect that they had cancelled my policy on account of a change in my occupations. However, they would accept me if I would except (sp) ½ of the regular benefit. I haven’t written them and will think it over for a day or two. They sent me a check for $1.25 which was coming to me. I made the man buy shades for the house. Shades don’t go with houses up here. He bought awful good wallpaper. Paid .65 a roll. He is fixing it up awful good all over. The rent is a litle high in this town, and I could find just a lot of houses I could get for from $5.00 to $8.00 a month less, but this one is going to be so handy and convenient, and that is worth something. Most of the houses for rent here are on the order of the Kiddoo house in Neosho. I think your only objection to this house is a pretty small kitchen, but the dining and living rooms make up for it. And the bedrooms are 11 X 13, and I think we should be more comfortable in this placed than we have ever been before. New get ready, Dearest, and get an early start Saturday, and don’t load yourself down with anything but what you just have to have. Send any thing you don’t need on the trip by Express from Joplin. It will get here on the same train if you get there early enough. Call all the taxies in town the evening before and find out if any of them are going to make an early trip. You might save 2 or 3 dollars. I could have made that train at Joplin the morning I left. We met Mr. Houff coming back at Reddings Mill. Am enclosing a check for $20.00 so you will not get stranded on the road. Be awful careful with the children, Honey. I know you will. I wish your Mother or Don could go with you to Joplin and see and help you on the train. I would be glad to pay their fare. I will close now and go and mail this. All my love to you all. Carl P.S. Came near writing a book, Honey, didn’t I? Chanute, Kansas June 3, 1924 My Dearest Hazel – children, I didn’t get a letter from you today, but I will write you a few lines anyway, for I know the days are pretty long for you and you are anxious to come. I went up to the house after supper and worked on the crating, pulling out all the nails, and I am going to put them up over the rafters in the garage and take care of it. I hope we will not have to do any crating soon, but there is over $20.00 worth of lumber if we had to buy it. I have been hoeing all day and I am a litle tired. It is pretty hard on a fellow’s back, but I like it, and I am feeling awful good. I will be awful glad when I get a real cook up here. I am awful anxious to see you and those babies. They are working on the house now, and it will be all ready when you get here. This is a pretty short letter, Dearest, but I will do better next time. Good night, Honey. Love to you all. Carl Chanute, Kansas June 4, 1924 My Dearest Hazel and children, Your nice letter came today and I can tell, Dear, that you are getting anxious to come. But you are not more anxious than I am to have you and the babies come, and the time is drawing near now. By tomorrow night, they will have all the floors sanded and paper hung, and Thrusday the chandelier and bath fixtures and kitchen cabinet will be put in, and I am sure by Friday it will be all completed. I put up the deposit for gas, electric and water today $15.00, and then I think I will stay home Saturday morning and arrange the furniture and clean the rugs and do a thousand and one litle things. I don’t know what to tell you about cherries, Dearest, but by the time you get this it will be too late to do any canning and you may be able to get some up here. They are just coming in now, and there seems to be lots of them here. I am going up to the house tomorrow afternoon and move all the furniture in to the back bedroom so they can finish the floors and the rest of the house. Then it will stay there until I move it back. I am going to close this now and go to bed, but I will write you again tomorrow, Dearest. All my love to you all. Carl Chanute, Kansas June 4, 1924 My Dearest Hazel – children, I am just back from the house. I go over there every evening after supper. I found some paint over there and I painted the white rocker, Max’s bed, the refrigerator and the kitchen table. I think they will almost finish up tomorrow. They have finished up the back bedroom and I will move the furniture in there in the a.m. before I go to work. I will not go to work Saturday, and go down and rent a vacuum cleaner and clean the rugs good before I lay them down and get the furniture moved around and try to have things in shape by the time you get here. This will be my last letter to you Honey, for I don’t think this will reach you until Friday, and I am going to cut this pretty short. I am going to write Mr. Nolan a letter tonight, although he owes me a letter. Will look for you Friday noon, Dearest. All my love to all of you. Carl
