Pentimenti - The Ghosts in the Painting Dear Brynn, Carly and Fallon: Imagine an artist painting a portrait. Maybe at first, they painted the person wearing a hat. But halfway through, the artist decided, "No, I don't like the hat," and they painted over it with hair or the background. When the painting is new, you can only see the final layer. But as the painting gets older—maybe a hundred years later—the paint on top starts to become a little see-through. Suddenly, you can see the faint outline of that old hat showing through the hair. That "ghost" of the old painting showing through the new one is called pentimento. It comes from an Italian word that means "to change your mind." It’s a clue that tells us the story of how the painting was made. We Are Living Paintings Now, think about you. You are the bright, colorful, finished painting that everyone sees today. But, just like that old canvas, you have layers underneath you. Those layers are the stories of our ancestors—your great-grandparents and the people who came before them. Even though they lived a long time ago, their "brushstrokes" still show through in your life. Here is how you can spot their pentimento in the real world: Where We Live (The Background): Have you ever wondered why we live in this town? It’s usually because of a decision someone made a long time ago. Maybe your Great-Grandpa moved here to find a job in a factory, or your Great-Grandma moved here to be close to the ocean. We are standing on this spot of earth because of a choice they made in the "underpainting" of our family history. Our Traditions (The Colors): Think about the funny things we do on holidays or the special way we cook Sunday dinner. Those aren't accidents! Those are habits passed down layer by layer. When we bake that special bread, we are repeating a motion someone did 100 years ago in a tiny kitchen in another country. It’s a little piece of the past showing through in our kitchen today. Your Personality (The Details): This is my favorite part. Sometimes, you do something—maybe the way you tap your foot when you're thinking, or how stubborn you get when you want to win a game—and it reminds me exactly of my father, or an aunt you never met. You have your own personality, of course! But those little tendencies? Those are the pentimenti. It’s the strength, the humor, or the talent of the people who came before you, shining through your eyes. Genealogy—which is just a fancy word for being a family detective—is how we look for those "pentimenti". When we find old records or photos, we aren't just looking at names and dates. We are trying to see the sketch underneath our lives. Knowing about the layers makes you more interesting. It means you aren't just one person standing alone; you are a masterpiece built on top of hundreds of stories, mistakes, adventures, and brave choices. With love and pride, OPA
